The opportunity

For a product like Joist, the customer journey doesn't end at signup. It moves through activation, the first useful result, repeat usage, paid conversion and retention. Each transition gives us something to understand and improve.

My working approach

Start with the customer behaviour. Then build the programme around it.

These are priorities I'd investigate with the EverPro team, not claims about Joist's current performance. I'd want to see the data and hear from the people closest to the product before deciding what to change.

01

Find the first moment of value

What does a new contractor need to accomplish after signing up to see that Joist is worth using? Identify the behaviours linked with early activation and time to value.

02

Make the next step obvious

Design journeys around behaviour, not just time elapsed: onboarding, estimating, invoicing, payment setup, feature discovery and relevant nudges.

03

Measure retention, not activity alone

Track movement between stages, holdout results where practical, repeat usage, paid conversion and churn indicators. Challenge programmes that get clicks without changing outcomes.

A focused first 90 days

Listen. Prioritize. Ship. Learn.

Days 1–30

Understand the journey

Review lifecycle objectives, existing flows, the customer event model, product constraints, segmentation, current experiments and operational ownership. Agree on meaningful measures with Product, Data and Growth.

Days 31–60

Choose the first improvements

Identify high-friction transitions and create a test plan with clear hypotheses, audiences, guardrails and feasibility. Focus on one or two areas where learning could inform the wider lifecycle programme.

Days 61–90

Launch and establish a rhythm

Partner with the team to ship selected journeys, monitor data quality and performance, document results and set a practical cadence for subsequent tests. Adjust based on what the evidence says.
