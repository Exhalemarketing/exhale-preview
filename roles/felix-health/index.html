Where patients slip away

Getting a patient is hard. Losing one is quiet.

Nobody decides to stop their care. It happens in small gaps along the way, like these.

  1. Health assessment
  2. An assessment is started and never finished
  3. First delivery
  4. The first box arrives, and the next message feels generic
  5. Renewal visit
  6. A renewal comes due and nobody nudges
  7. Ongoing care
  8. A patient pauses, and never hears from you again

Each of these is a moment for a short, kind message at the right time. That's what a lifecycle program is for.

The first stretch

What I'd do in the first 90 days.

Here's the work I'd actually do in the first three months, alongside your people.

  1. Days 1 to 30

    Listen and map

    I read every message a patient gets today, transactional and marketing, in the order they arrive. I sit with product, design, engineering and analytics, and look at where patients drop between assessment, first delivery and renewal. We pick the leak that, if we fix it first, keeps the most patients in their care.

  2. Days 31 to 60

    Fix the first leaks

    I rebuild the first journeys in Iterable, starting with the biggest drop. Each change runs as a clear test against a control. Someone on your side approves anything that goes to patients.

  3. Days 61 to 90

    Make it a rhythm

    A monthly lifecycle review is running, with a test log the whole team can read. Win-back journeys are live for patients who've paused. Everything is written down, so the work doesn't live in one person's head.
