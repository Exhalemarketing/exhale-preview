From your RFP

What the RFP asks for, and how I'd approach it.

A fundraising assessment

Three to five years of your records, 8 to 12 confidential interviews with staff, Board members and ASRC stakeholders, and a short written assessment. What's working, what isn't, and where staff time pays back best.

A practical multi-year plan

Which channels to grow, keep, change or stop, with annual goals and early signs you can track before the money arrives. Corporate and subsidiary partnerships, a grants approach, and clear roles for staff, Board and partners.

Training and coaching for staff

Six working sessions for the AEF Director and your development lead, built around your real prospects and sponsors, with materials you keep.

Tools that outlast the engagement

A prospect and sponsorship tracker, a stewardship calendar, contact plans, solicitation and thank-you templates, and a simple dashboard, all in tools you already have.

Donor tracking without a CRM

A clean, simple tracker first. Then a recommendation on whether and when a donor database is worth it, with honest costs.

Where gifts slip away

Getting a donor is hard. Losing one is quiet.

Nobody decides to let a donor go. It happens at small gaps along the way, like these.

  1. First gift
  2. The thank-you comes late, or not at all
  3. Second gift
  4. Nobody asks again
  5. Monthly giving
  6. A monthly gift lapses, and no one follows up
  7. Years of giving
  8. They lapse, and never hear from you

Fewer than 1 in 5 first-time donors give again. Every one who drifts away is a relationship you worked hard to start.

Questions

Is this the proposal?

What about the development position you're planning?

That person is who this work is for. If they're in place by spring, they'd join the training from the start. If not, they'd inherit the plan, the tools and the training materials.

Do you work in our office?

Mostly by video, from Peachland in British Columbia, as the addendum describes. If an on-site visit would help, I'm glad to come to Alaska. It's optional, billed at cost, and only booked with your approval.

Will you solicit donors or write grant applications for us?

No, and the addendum says you don't expect that. I'd help your staff prepare for real conversations and apply the tools to real opportunities, so the training sticks.

You haven't worked with Alaska Native organizations. How would you handle that?

By saying so, and by listening first. I'd ask your staff to guide how interviews are introduced, who should be asked, and what stays inside AEF. The plan should sound like AEF, and that comes from your people.

We're a small team. Is this only for big organizations?

No. I've worked with large international teams and small ones. The fix is matched to the size of the problem, and to your budget.

Do we need new software?

Usually not. I start with what you already pay for, like your CRM, email tool and shared drives, and make them work harder.

Will you need access to our donor data?

Only what we agree on, inside tools you approve. Please don't send donor or program details by email. We'll set up access properly if we work together.

Can you guarantee results?

No, and be wary of anyone who does. What I can promise is honest advice and regular check-ins, so you always know where things stand.

Who would we contract with?

Exhale Marketing, my company in British Columbia, as a consultant. I'd do the work myself.

Do you work outside Canada?

Yes. I work remotely with organizations in Canada, the US and internationally.
